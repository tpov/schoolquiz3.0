- (note) BROWNFIELD, decisive: the market is ALREADY BUILT. functions/index.js:989 (logos) and :1165 (nicknames) each run one Firestore transaction that checks ownership, checks buyer gold, splits the price and moves item, payment and listing together. The spec's CAP-5 does not need designing, it needs ratifying.
- (note) BROWNFIELD: splitSalePrice(price, commissionPercent) takes the commission OUT of the listed price — splitSalePrice(100,10) returns {total:100, commission:10, sellerGets:90}. The buyer pays the listed price; the seller receives less. spec-monetisation CAP-5 says the opposite: buyer pays price plus 5%, seller receives the full price. Direct conflict, and it is the first coaching fork.
- (note) BROWNFIELD: the commission rate is already a server knob, policy.saleCommissionPercent in configs/nickname_policy, with a DEFAULT_SALE_COMMISSION_PERCENT and a clamp. MIN_LISTING_PRICE=1. Nickname asking prices are already tiered: 10 gold for <=4 chars, free for the first >=6-char name, 1 gold otherwise.
- (note) BROWNFIELD: splitSalePrice deliberately gives the rounding remainder to the seller so the two halves add back to the price exactly — the author's comment notes that rounding each independently would mint or destroy a coin per sale. That is a conservation property the architecture must preserve.
- (decision) AD candidate — commission is taken OUT of the listed price, ratifying splitSalePrice. Buyer pays exactly the price shown; seller receives price minus saleCommissionPercent; the rounding remainder goes to the seller so the halves add back to the price exactly. Binds CAP-5. Prevents a second pricing convention appearing beside the shipped one, and prevents the per-sale minting a naive independent rounding would cause. Owner chose this over the spec's price-plus-5% after both were laid out.
- (direction) Owner's working instruction, standing: no code in replies, short plain-language summaries, decide the small things myself and keep going — he will answer the same way regardless. Coaching mode ends here; the rest of the spine is authored and reported, not elicited.
- (decision) AD — escrow at bid time. Placing a bid debits the gold immediately; losing bids are refunded at close and the winning bid is destroyed. Owner chose this over leader-only escrow and over charging at close. Prevents the uncollectable winning bid and the second-bid cascade that would follow it.
- (decision) AD — the receipt pattern replaces batch payout. The server credits the balance itself and writes an addPoints-shaped envelope (amounts plus a human message) onto the profile; the client only displays and clears it. Reuses the legacy mechanism the owner already proved. Prevents the double-credit the legacy version had, where a lost clear made the client re-apply, and satisfies the inherited rule that the client never moves a server-protected balance.
- (decision) AD — one catalogue. ShopItemId is the single shelf; real-money items are ordinary entries on it carrying ShopCurrency.EXTERNAL plus a Play SKU. StoreProductId stops being a second product list and becomes only the SKU mapping. Prevents a shelf that offers what the store cannot sell, and a store selling what the shelf never shows.
- (decision) AD — purchase verification is its own synchronous callable, not a deferred mutation through the shared receiver. It depends on Play's answer, which is state the client does not have, so the inherited AD-3 puts it on the synchronous side. Prevents a purchase sitting in the offline queue while the player waits for gold that cannot arrive offline.
- (decision) AD — the season is frozen by one small scheduled job that writes the final top-100 into a season document; every payout after that is credit-plus-receipt. Prevents a long-running batch over all users and the retry semantics that come with it.
- (decision) AD — premium is an expiry timestamp owned by the server. Every route (money, gold, box drop, manual grant) adds duration to the remaining time rather than replacing it. Prevents four acquisition paths each inventing their own extension rule.
- (decision) AD — the ad SDK never reaches a feature module. Rewarded-ad completion enters the domain as a signed server-verified event, not as an SDK callback a Compose screen observes. Prevents the ad vendor's types spreading through presentation and prevents a client claiming a reward the network never granted.
